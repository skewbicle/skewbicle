5x5 (Professor's Cube)

The smallest "odd" layered big cube. It combines fixed centers with edge-pairing techniques from the 4x4, but has no center-edge parities.

Before starting, get familiar with 5x5 move notation :

  • Rw / Rw': Move the two rightmost layers together clockwise / counter-clockwise
  • Uw / Uw': Move the two top layers together clockwise / counter-clockwise
  • Lw / Lw': Move the two leftmost layers together clockwise / counter-clockwise
  • 3Rw: Move the outer 3 layers on the right side together
  • x: Tilt the cube forward (the bottom face moves to face front)

Step 1 — White Center

Build the white 3x3 center grid by making three 1x3 bars.

  • Middle Bar

    • Pair the white center piece with 2 White inner-edges to make a 1x3 line.

  • Side Bars

    • Make two separate 1x3 bars on adjacent faces (1 corner-center + 1 edge-center + 1 corner-center).

  • Assemble

    • Slide the side bars onto the White face to form a complete 3x3 block.

Step 2 — Yellow Center

Flip the cube so White is on the bottom.

  • Middle Bar

    • Build a yellow bar on the top face while preserving the white center using: Rw and U

  • Second Bar

    • Build a yellow bar and move it to the top face while preserving the white center using: Rw U2 Rw'

  • Last Bar

    • Make the last yellow bar and align it under an existing top bar to finish the 3x3 yellow center by also using: Rw U2 Rw'

Step 3 — Side Centers

With White on the left and Yellow on the right, solve the 4 middle centers around the sides.

  • First 2 Centers

    • Solve the first two centers using the Rw U2 Rw' bar-insertion logic.

  • Last 2 Centers

    • We need to make only two corner center pieces remain to be swapped by using: Lw' U2 Lw U Lw' U Lw.

Step 4 — Edge Pairing

  • Free Pairing (First 8 Edges)

    • Place matching edges on the front-left and front-right slots and check if they line up or not.

  • If they line up

    • Slice inner layers to form a complete 3-piece edge → Store it in the top layer → Replace it with an unsolved edge → Unslice to fix centers.

  • If they don't line up

    • Apply the Flipping Algorithm: R U R' F R' F' R

    Then slice inner layers to form a complete 3-piece edge → Store it in the top layer → Replace it with an unsolved edge → Unslice to fix centers.

  • Last 4 Edges

    • When no unsolved top/bottom edges remain, place matching pieces on the same horizontal row and use slicing and flipping turns to join them directly without breaking centers.

Step 5 — Fix The Parity (If Needed)

A 5x5 Rubik's cube edge parity happens when pairing the last edges and one single edge piece remains flipped or mismatched.

Rw U2 x Rw U2 Rw U2 3Rw' U2 Lw U2 Rw' U2 Rw U2 Rw' U2 Rw'

Step 6 — Solve Like a 3x3

Turn only the outer layers (R, L, U, D, F, B) and solve the rest of the cube like a standard 3x3!
